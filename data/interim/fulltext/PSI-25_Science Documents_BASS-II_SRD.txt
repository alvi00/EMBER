BASS-II SCIENCE REQUIREMENTS
•
General Requirements
–
20 samples for each of the 5 PIs will be flow (including 20 clear pmma rods, 20 
black pmma rods (varied diameters for both); 19 SIBAL samples, 1 4 cm pmma 
spherical section,   20 pmma films (varied thicknesses) and 20 pmma slabs (varied 
thicknesses and 1 sided or 2 sided holders).  Also 3 Nomex-III samples.
–
27 additional igniter wires will be flown.  Solid fuel ignition uses a Kanthal hot wire.
– ISS nitrogen gas will be used (0 to 500 SCCM N2).  Instead of connecting the 
Experiment Assembly to a gas bottle it is connected via the GN2 hose to the MSG 
GN2 interface.  GN2 is used to vitiate the MSG working volume to desired O2 levels.
–
8 additional flash cards will be flow for the still camera.
•
Parameter Measurement
– Measurement of the GN2 flow rate, fan voltage, air flow velocity and direction, 
and flame radiation will be measured and overlaid on the Video.
– The acquisition rate of 4 Hz can not be changed from SPICE/BASS.
– The measurement accuracy of ±10% was met by the SPICE hardware prior to 
its launch.
•
Imaging Requirements
– The field of view of the video can not be changed from BASS.
– The D300s still image requirements are the same as for BASS.

=== PAGE 2 ===
BASS-II SCIENCE REQUIREMENTS
•
Acceleration
– BASS-II testing will be scheduled around planned thruster firing or 
spacecraft dock or undock.
– The acceleration environment will be measured by a nearby SAMS 
during BASS-II testing.
•
MSG Working Volume Gas Concentrations
–
Project will include steps in crew procedures to install a Compound Specific 
Analyzer (CSA-CP) and the CO2 sensor (CDM) into the MSG to measure the 
Oxygen concentration.  These devices will provide readings before and after 
each burn of the MSG working volume well mixed gas concentrations.  
Calibration checks will utilize the Columbus and Node 3 O2 and CO2 readings.
•
Crew support
–
Crew time is needed to conduct the BASS-II experiments.  As part of the nominal 
ops, the crew establishes the desired test ambient atmosphere and flow.  The 
crew  manually inserts  the fuel sample, adjusts the fuel location and igniter 
position within the camera fields of view.   The crew manually ignites the sample 
and triggers the still camera.   The crew performs flow calibration and soot 
cleaning as needed.  
–
The crew works closely with the BASS-II PI team to determine test settings and 
provide real-time feedback during each burn.

BASS-II SUCCESS CRITERIA
•
Minimal Success: 10 samples from each PI (of their total 20) 
will be successfully tested per PI matrices  at the desired test 
conditions (O2, flow).  Ignition is successful for these tests, 
and flame video data is obtained.  Still camera data is 
obtained for at least 7 tests per PI.  Valuable information 
informing SoFIE science requirements is obtained.
•
Complete Success: All PI samples (100) are tested at the 
desired test conditions.  Video data is obtained for all tests.  
Ignition occurs in most of the tests (90+).  Extinction is 
observed for tests that seek to find a flammability limit.  Most 
of the tests (90+) provide still camera data. Valuable 
information informing SoFIE science requirements is 
obtained.

BASS-II Science Overview
Relevance/Impact:  Burning and Suppression of Solids - II (BASS-II) tests the hypothesis 
that materials in microgravity, with adequate ventilation, burn as well if not better than 
the same material in normal gravity with other conditions being identical (pressure, 
oxygen concentration, temperature, etc.).  
Objectives:  
•
To identify what is the worst case for material flammability in spacecraft 
environments, and how does that compare to the terrestrial upward burning used to 
screen the materials for safe use aboard spacecraft.
•
To determine how quickly a fire in space can grow and if the flames reach a finite size 
or continue to grow.  This has implications for firefighting strategies in spacecraft.
Primary variables:  The main variables to be tested are the effects of ambient oxygen 
concentration, ventilation flow velocity, and fuel type, thickness, and geometry.  
Development Approach:
BASS-II will utilize the on orbit BASS hardware launched originally for SPICE on STS-126.  
New samples, igniters, and more flash cards will provide new data.  Crew is required to 
set up and operate the experiment.  Video and data down-linked to the ground for 
evaluation.
